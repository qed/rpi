Missing Middle Housing Opportunities in Canada’s Hottest Housing Markets

Missing middle housing refers to housing types that fall between single-family homes and high-rise apartments—such as duplexes, triplexes, townhouses, and low-rise multiplexes. These housing forms are increasingly scarce in Canada’s most in-demand housing markets like Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, Calgary, and Ottawa, contributing to affordability and supply issues.

Challenges Identified:

  1. Zoning Restrictions: Most urban residential land is zoned exclusively for single-family homes, preventing the development of more diverse and affordable options.

  2. Political and Community Resistance: NIMBYism and local opposition to densification often hinder new developments.

  3. Financial Barriers: Developers face high land costs, limited financing for small-scale infill projects, and complex approval processes.

  4. Infrastructure Limitations: Aging infrastructure in many cities isn't always equipped to support higher densities.

Opportunities and Solutions:

  • Zoning Reform: Cities are beginning to update zoning bylaws to allow for duplexes, triplexes, and fourplexes in traditionally single-family areas (e.g., Toronto’s “Multiplex Initiative”).

  • Streamlined Approvals: Simplifying permitting and approval processes for missing middle projects can reduce delays and costs.

  • Public-Private Partnerships: Collaborations between governments and developers can help deliver more affordable middle housing.

  • Incentives and Grants: Offering financial support or tax breaks for infill and gentle density projects encourages more developers to enter the space.

Potential Impacts:

  • Increased Housing Supply: Gentle densification in urban cores can significantly boost the housing stock without drastic changes to neighborhood character.

  • Improved Affordability: Diverse housing types provide more affordable ownership and rental options for middle-income Canadians.

  • Stronger Communities: Missing middle housing fosters mixed-income, multi-generational neighborhoods with better access to services and transit.
