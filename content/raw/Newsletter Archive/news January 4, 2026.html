2025 reset the Toronto real estate market. Here’s what matters in 2026.   
 

2025 wasn’t a strong market by traditional measures. Sales were down and sentiment was weak. But that weakness is exactly what created opportunity for the right type of real estate investor.
 
Both prices and rents came down in 2025, but deal prices fell more than rents. When that happens, rent yields improve. At the same time, mortgage rates began to come down. That’s why monthly cash flow on well-bought properties was some of the strongest we’ve seen in years.
 
From a housing policy standpoint, 2025 quietly became one of the most important years Toronto has seen for the multi-family space:
  • Sixplexes are now permitted in nine wards.
  • Development charges are waived for up to six units.
  • Rules around garden suites are simplified, making backyard projects more viable.
  • The Major Streets appeal was thrown out, opening the door to mid-rise density.
  • Recent LTB changes have also started to bring more balance back to the rental market. 
These materially change what investors can create and execute in this city.
 
Interest rates have come down, which helps. But the market is still slow, and just hoping prices go up isn’t the right real estate strategy.
 
What works now is buying properties that pay you well every month. Prices are more realistic, risk is lower, and returns come from cash flow, not just waiting for appreciation.
 
This isn’t about going all-in on real estate. It’s about thoughtfully adding it to a balanced investment strategy now that the risk profile has improved.
