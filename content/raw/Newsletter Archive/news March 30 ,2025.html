Commonly Overlooked Tax Deductions for Canadian Real Estate Investors (2024)

As the April 30th deadline approaches, real estate investors should ensure they're maximizing available tax deductions. Here are several frequently overlooked opportunities to reduce your tax burden:

Property-Related Deductions

Interest Expenses: Beyond mortgage interest, remember that interest on loans for property improvements and lines of credit used for property-related expenses are deductible. Maintain proper records linking borrowed funds to investment purposes.

Depreciation (CCA): Consider claiming Capital Cost Allowance on rental buildings (4% for most properties), appliances (20%), and furniture (20%). While this reduces your adjusted cost base, strategic CCA claims can defer tax liabilities. Note: that if your property is in a high-growth area, you may want to skip this deduction on your real estate.

Legal and Accounting: Fees for investment-related tax advice, property-related legal services, and accounting services are fully deductible. This includes costs for preparing rental statements or handling tenant disputes.

Property Management: Fees paid to property managers are deductible business expenses, including finding tenants, collecting rent, and arranging repairs.

Ontario-Specific Considerations

Land Transfer Tax Rebate: First-time homebuyers in Ontario can receive a rebate of up to $4,000 on provincial land transfer tax, and Toronto residents can receive an additional municipal rebate of up to $4,475.

Remember to consult with a qualified tax professional to ensure your specific situation is optimized. The landscape of allowable deductions changes annually, and personalized advice is crucial for maximizing your returns.
