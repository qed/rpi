Bank of Canada holds interest rate for third time at 2.75%

 Best. Be Your Best.

The Bank of Canada has maintained its key interest rate at 2.75 per cent for the third consecutive time, indicating a cautious approach amid ongoing economic uncertainty. However, the Bank has hinted at the possibility of future rate cuts, contingent on inflation pressures easing. Any decision to lower the policy interest rate would depend on the balance between downward inflationary forces from a weakening economy and upward pressures resulting from tariff-related costs and global trade realignments.

 

Three primary factors influenced the Bank's decision to hold rates steady: persistent uncertainty surrounding U.S. trade policy, signs of resilience in the Canadian economy, and emerging underlying inflation pressures. Unlike previous updates, the Bank chose not to release a traditional economic forecast in its latest monetary policy report. Instead, it presented three hypothetical scenarios reflecting different potential outcomes for trade policy and economic conditions.

 

In the first scenario, which assumes current tariffs remain unchanged, sectoral tariffs are still applied to specific goods like autos, steel, and aluminum. Most goods traded under the Canada-United States-Mexico Agreement (CUSMA) remain tariff-free, but retaliatory tariffs from Canada on $60 billion worth of U.S. goods are still in place. Under this model, the Bank expects economic contraction in the second quarter of 2025, followed by a modest recovery, with GDP growth reaching 1.8 per cent by 2027 as household spending and exports stabilize.

 

The second scenario outlines a tariff escalation, where Canada and Mexico lose their CUSMA tariff exemptions, and the U.S. imposes steep tariffs on goods, including a 50 per cent levy on copper. In response, Canada increases its own retaliatory tariffs. This scenario also incorporates a worsening U.S.-China trade war. Economic growth contracts throughout the remainder of 2025, with only a slow recovery starting in early 2026. Inflation rises above 2.5 per cent by the third quarter of 2026.

Conversely, the third scenario explores a de-escalation of trade tensions. Sectoral tariffs are cut in half, and Canada lifts its retaliatory tariffs. In this environment, the Canadian economy grows by about two per cent in the latter half of 2025 and maintains an average growth rate of 1.7 per cent through 2027. Inflation remains below the Bank's two per cent target until late 2026.

 

Currently, underlying inflation sits at 2.5 per cent—above the headline rate of 1.9 per cent—largely due to the removal of the carbon tax and rising costs for imported goods like vehicles, furniture, and coffee. This uptick in prices stems from the Canadian dollar’s depreciation in late 2024, higher past shipping costs, and earlier increases in agricultural prices.

 

The Bank also anticipates a rebound in residential investment during the second half of 2025, following an 11 per cent decline earlier in the year. However, weak business investment and slow population growth are expected to continue limiting the country’s long-term economic potential.

 

Concluding Summary:

The Bank of Canada is maintaining its cautious stance on interest rates while monitoring the complex interplay of domestic resilience, inflation, and global trade uncertainty. While the current rate remains unchanged, future adjustments will depend on how economic conditions evolve under different trade policy scenarios. The Bank’s flexible approach reflects the challenges of forecasting in an unpredictable global environment.
